01 Thesis
i

The records exist. The understanding does not.

A family's history is already written down. It is written down in three mailboxes, several drives, two photo libraries, a laptop, and the dumps of the laptops before it. Every one of those accounts is owned by a company that indexes it for its own purposes, and none of them know the others exist. The information is not missing. It is unreconciled.

ii

One model, held locally.

LifeOS reads those sources and builds a single model above them: people, places, events, properties, institutions, documents, obligations, tax years. Twenty files that are one document become one document with twenty pieces of provenance. The model is cross referenced, versioned, and correctable by a human, and it lives on hardware its subject owns.

iii

The honest scope.

This is a personal experiment on one family's archive, built slowly, over years, in public. Two layers run today and are measured. The reasoning layer above them is design work with prototypes behind it, and is labelled that way everywhere on this page. Nothing here is a product, and there is nothing to sign up for.

LifeOS does not organize files. It organizes reality inferred from evidence.

02 The architecture

Six layers. Each one throws away less than the last.

Every layer takes the layer below it as raw material and refuses to discard what it cannot yet explain. Uncertainty travels upward with the data instead of being rounded off at each step.

What should exist? What changed? What is inconsistent? What deserves attention?

The first question is search, and search has been solved for thirty years. The second set requires a system that holds a model of what a complete life record looks like, compares it against what it has actually observed, and can say something useful about the difference. That gap is the whole project.

03 What has been processed

The hard problem is not storage. It is deciding when twenty files are one thing.

89,569Fragments cataloged

77,078 photo and file records across eight sources, plus 12,491 document occurrences across seven more. Every one of them is a claim about something, and almost none of them are unique.

12,491 → 7,703Occurrences into logical documents

Twelve thousand scattered occurrences became seven thousand seven hundred logical records. The same tax document arriving as an attachment, saved to a drive, re-saved by a spouse, and scanned again years later is one document with four pieces of provenance, not four documents.

6,884Clusters checksums could not see

Visual duplicate groups whose members have different bytes and identical content: a recompressed copy sitting beside its original. Collapsing them refined 56,943 checksum-unique files down to roughly 45,325 photographs that actually exist. The collection was smaller, and more precious, than the byte count claimed.

164.8 GBRedundant, and still on disk

Identified, marked, reported, and left exactly where it was. The analysis is marks-only. Nothing in the pipeline has ever deleted an original, and no part of it is permitted to.

890 · 18,369Entities and document links

The graph that makes the rest possible. Every link is a typed edge between a document and something in the world, which is what allows a question about a person, a year or an institution to be answered without a filename ever being involved.

3,678Documents recovered by OCR

Records that existed only as photographs of paper. Alongside them, 11 extraction failures recorded as failures, because a document that could not be read is a different thing from a document that is not there, and collapsing the two would corrupt every conclusion about absence.

9,275 · 35,344Messages scanned, attachments triaged

Three mailboxes read with a read-only OAuth scope, from which 10,879 attachments were worth fetching and the rest were cataloged and left alone. Knowing that a thing exists is often the whole job.

23Records that should exist and do not

Across fifteen monitored record series, six show breaks in a pattern that had been regular for years. Nobody asked for those records. The system worked out what a complete series looks like and reported the holes, which is the first thing on this page that search cannot do.

04 The two instruments

Both are running. Neither is a mock.

Lifegram

Photo intelligence layer

Reconciles every photo library the family owns into one master set, then learns the people, places and events inside it, entirely on local hardware.

  • Multi-source dedup by checksum and by perceptual hash, with one canonical copy chosen per cluster
  • Face detection and clustering into people, with a human review loop that outranks the model
  • Offline reverse geocoding, home and trip detection, and semantic search over image embeddings

~45,325Distinct photos
53,780Faces detected

Perceptual hashing found the collection was smaller, and more precious, than the byte count claimed.

Docugram

Document intelligence layer

Catalogs every document the family has, works out which ones are the same document, extracts what they are about, and reasons about what is missing.

  • Exact and fuzzy dedup with version chains, so drafts of one document stay attached to it
  • OCR, entity extraction and a knowledge graph linking documents to people, years and institutions
  • Advisory retention, missing-record analysis, and recoverable-value leads that always end at a human

Extraction failures are recorded as failures. Absence is a conclusion, and it has to be earned.

M02

Records continuity

What should still exist, but disappeared?

Documentary patterns that recur on their own schedule get monitored for breaks. A series that arrived every year for six years and then stopped is a finding, not an absence of results. This is absence reasoning, and it is the opposite of search.

Expected→ Observed→ Missing
M03

Dormant value

What might my history have left behind?

Evidence chains run from a former employer to a plan administrator to the absence of any rollover evidence, and produce a lead a human then verifies with the institution. The system never says money is owed. It says here is a chain, and here is the gap in it.

Observed→ Reconciled→ Verified by a human
M04

Life contradictions

Where does my own history disagree with itself?

Two policies that both look active. A date that appears twice with different values. Three files each claiming to be the latest version. Each conflict resolves into historical truth, current truth, or an honest unresolved contradiction that stays visible. A personal truth-maintenance system.

Observed→ Conflict→ Resolved or declared
M05

Family continuity

If my family suddenly needed to understand everything important, could they?

A structured map of what exists, who it belongs to, who depends on it, and what is missing, generated from evidence rather than assembled by hand from decades of folders. The archive a family inherits should not be a password nobody wrote down.

Expected→ Observed→ Verified
M06

Home as entity

What has this house actually cost, and what has no maintenance evidence?

A property that accumulates its own history: purchase, insurance, taxes, repairs, warranties, and the appliance whose warranty has an installation date and no service record since. A place stops being an address and becomes an entity with a ledger.

Observed→ Reconciled→ Missing
M07

Transition detection

Detect life's chapters instead of asking for them.

An address change, a shift in photo geography, a closing document and an insurance change all landing in the same season are one event seen from four angles. The system proposes the chapter; one human confirmation mints it.

Converging evidence→ Confirmed by a human
M08

The unfinished things

What started but never concluded?

Applications without outcomes. Claims without resolutions. Deposits without refunds. Each is a pattern of initiated, then expected evidence, then nothing, and the useful signal is the silence at the end rather than any document in the chain.

Initiated→ Expected→ Absent→ Unresolved
M09

Personal provenance

Why does LifeOS believe this?

Every assertion is inspectable down to the evidence that produced it, including the confidence and the human corrections applied along the way. The system does not state truth. It shows its receipts and lets a person disagree with them.

Photographs, the people in them, the coordinates, the reservation confirmation and the receipts collapse into one event object with a beginning, an end, a cast and a cost. A gallery shows you files from a week. This returns the week.

07 Field notes

Things that only became obvious after they broke.

Written while building, in the order the lessons arrived. Every number below is a real measurement from this archive.

N01

The sixteen-thousand-face blob

I merged similar face clusters with union-find, which quietly makes merging transitive: if A resembles B and B resembles C, A and C end up in one person even when they look nothing alike. At six thousand faces this was invisible. At 53,780 it produced a single cluster holding 16,504 faces, and five clusters between them held sixty percent of every face in the library.

Merging is now one hop only, and only between clusters that are each other's nearest neighbour. The bug was not the threshold. The bug was an operation that composed.

Lesson: a similarity relation is not an equivalence relation, and treating it as one fails silently until it fails enormously.

N02

The age model that got deleted

The face toolkit ships an age estimator, so I used it. Then I measured it against four people whose ages I knew exactly. It was wrong by 11 to 26 years in every case: a three-year-old read as forty-five, a forty-year-old read as twenty-three. It was squashing everything toward its training mean. Re-cropping to the exact framing the model expected did not help.

It was not demoted to a hint or shown with a confidence badge. It was removed from the pipeline. Age now comes from birth dates a human typed in, which also made the slowest stage faster.

Lesson: a measured-wrong signal shown as a hint is still a wrong signal, and it will be believed.

N03

Thresholds are measured, not chosen

Every constant in the clustering path was originally a number that felt right for the library size I happened to have. Now the clustering threshold rises until no large cluster is still internally loose, and the auto-link threshold was validated against clusters I had labelled by hand, where it made zero errors.

Its ceiling is set by the hardest real case in the archive: two siblings photographed as babies score 0.763 against each other, higher than any two unrelated people. The hardest true case in your own data is the only honest place to put a limit.

Lesson: a constant nobody measured is a guess with a decimal point.

N04

Over-splitting is the safe direction

A person split across three clusters costs the reviewer two merge questions, answered in four seconds. Two people merged into one silently mis-attributes thousands of photographs and is very hard to notice, because the gallery looks fine.

Those two failures are not symmetric, so the thresholds are not centred. Every one of them is deliberately set to fail toward the cheap mistake.

Lesson: tune to the cost of being wrong, not to the rate of being wrong.

N05

Show the whole cluster, not its best faces

The review card sampled the twelve sharpest crops from a cluster, which is exactly what you would do if you wanted the reviewer to say yes. A cluster of 1,650 faces containing several people looked like one child, and I confidently named it.

Cards now draw half their crops from across the whole cluster, including the ugly ones. A review interface that shows you the model's best work is measuring the wrong thing.

Lesson: if you are asking a human to catch errors, show them where errors would live.

N06

Missing is not nonexistent

Eleven documents in the catalog failed text extraction. They are recorded as extraction failures, not as documents with no text, and the missing-record analysis refuses to reason over them.

This matters more than it sounds. The entire value of the absence reasoning depends on the difference between "this record is not here" and "I could not read this record". Collapse those two and every conclusion about a gap becomes noise.

Lesson: absence is a conclusion, and a conclusion needs evidence like any other.

N07

Checksums lie about photographs

6,884 duplicate clusters merge files with different checksums and identical visual content: a cloud-recompressed copy sitting next to the original it was made from. To a checksum these are unrelated files. To a person they are the same photograph, twice.

The other direction was funnier. The most-copied image in the entire library, at 34 copies, turned out to be a user-interface icon from a code repository. Which is precisely why project directories are no longer part of the photo library. Deciding what is not in the corpus is as much of the work as processing what is.

Lesson: identity is a property of content, and content is not bytes.

N08

Read-only at the driver, not at the discipline

The intelligence layer opens the source catalog with SQLite in read-only mode, so an accidental write fails at the driver rather than depending on my remembering not to. All derived state lives in a separate database keyed by the original identifiers.

Same principle in the mailboxes: the OAuth scope is read-only, so the code cannot send or delete an email even if it were asked to. The guarantee lives in the structure and not in an intention.

Lesson: make the dangerous thing impossible, not discouraged.

N09

A folder date is not a date

Around 6,200 photographs have no usable capture metadata, and their date was recovered from a folder name. Every one of those collapses to January 1 of its year.

Fed into event detection, that produces the largest New Year's party in recorded history, every year, for a decade. They are grouped as an undated batch instead, and marked as such, because a date whose precision you invented is worse than no date.

Lesson: carry the precision of a value alongside the value, or something downstream will assume it.

N10

The scripture false positive

A phrase matcher looking for uncashed cheques flagged a religious text with high confidence. The phrase "void after" appears in cheque boilerplate and, it turns out, in theology.

Nothing was harmed, because that analysis produces leads for a human rather than conclusions. But it is a useful reminder about which class of system this is: a pattern that fires on a phrase does not know what a document is about, and any pipeline that treats a keyword hit as a finding will eventually embarrass itself.

Lesson: precision is a discipline, not a default, and lead generation must always end at a person.

08 The trust model

A system that knows this much about a family has to be able to justify itself.

Source before assertion

Nothing is stated without a path back to the evidence that produced it.

Human truth outranks inference

An answer a person gave wins permanently, and survives a full rebuild of everything derived.

Unknown is a valid state

The model is allowed to hold a question open. A confident guess is more expensive than a gap.

Missing is not nonexistent

A failure to read is recorded as a failure to read, and never counted as an absence.

Originals remain originals

Duplicates are marked, reported, and left in place. No stage in either pipeline can delete a source file.

Nothing silently disappears

Every rebuild carries forward human corrections, and every correction is keyed to something stable.

Local when practical

Perception runs on this laptop at near-zero marginal cost, which is what makes running it over everything affordable.

Smallest sufficient intelligence

Frontier reasoning is spent only where it earns its price. Most of this problem is not a reasoning problem.

An AI that knows everything about a family, and nothing leaves the laptop.

01

The face, photo and document models are ONNX files on disk. There is no inference endpoint. Zero cloud calls are made to classify a photograph, embed a document, or recognise a face.

02

The review application binds to 127.0.0.1. It is not exposed to a network interface, so there is no deployment in which it is accidentally reachable.

03

Mailbox access is read-only at the OAuth scope. The permission to send or delete was never granted, so the capability does not exist to misuse.

04

The source catalog is opened read-only at the SQLite driver. A write fails at the driver rather than at somebody's discipline.

This is the one architectural claim that could not be copied by a cloud product, and it is the reason the whole experiment is worth running. A system this personal is only acceptable if the sovereignty is structural.
