A life is more
than a folder.

LifeOS brings the things you keep closer to the reasons you kept them.

A photograph has a person, a place, a moment. A document has a purpose, a date, a connection to something else. LifeOS explores a more useful way to move through those relationships.

01 One archive, several ways in
i

Follow a person

Start with someone who matters. Narrow the places and moments to the photographs they actually appear in. Combine people to find shared memories.

ii

Move through time

Browse a visual timeline, explore a place, or open a film. The same archive can answer different questions without being copied into more folders.

iii

Find the detail

Search extracted document text and narrow by year, source, format, or recognized context. See duplicate copies together and read a document without losing your place.

02 Built from two working projects

Lifegram meets Docugram.

Lifegram catalogs photographs and videos, with existing metadata and locally inferred people, places, and events. Docugram indexes documents and extracted text. LifeOS provides a shared browsing experience over both catalogs.

The current interface has connected filters, multiple selections, timelines, visual collections, document previews, and supported video playback. Photo search currently uses filenames and indexed sidecar text; it is not a general conversational search engine. Inferred labels can be wrong, and local review remains part of the workflow.

03 Private by design, explicit about the boundaries

The source stays close.

The archive lives on the owner’s laptop. Remote access requires an approved owner identity and multifactor authentication through Cloudflare Access. A tunnel connects to a read-only gateway on the laptop. When the laptop or its services are off, the archive is unavailable.

Cloudflare terminates HTTPS and proxies the requested content. This is not end-to-end encryption against Cloudflare. The public ThirdOcular website contains no archive files, and access to LifeOS does not grant access to SeshuLegacy. Original document downloads remain disabled until specific folders are allowed.

04 What comes next

A useful prototype earns its next chapter.

The next work is better metadata review, more expressive ways to explore connections, and dependable laptop startup and recovery. A unified search across photo meaning and document meaning remains a research direction. LifeOS is currently a personal working prototype, not a public subscription or a promise of universal archive intelligence.
