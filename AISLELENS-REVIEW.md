# AisleLens assessment — 2026-10-27

Recommendation: keep the product, but make the next investment a small paid customer
validation exercise. Do not restart broad feature development or return to generic AI
mention tracking. There is a plausible agency QA business here, not proven demand.

## Evidence reviewed

The current product already publishes versioned buying criteria, source evidence and
repeatable per-requirement results. Its live demo uses a frozen July 27 capture, not a
fresh observation of the merchant. Local ShopifyACO documentation describes working
tests, a Shopify app, a scan funnel and test-mode billing; commercial activation was not
reverified. No current revenue, retention or customer usage data was accessed.

The live public surface still carries multiple stories: an agency QA homepage, a legacy
AI Visibility Index, and older visibility wording on privacy material. That makes the
offer harder to understand. The indexed `/app` error/sample state is a lead to investigate,
not evidence that an authenticated customer session is broken.

The demo publishes seven incorrect positive results among 160 audited positive rows in
its coffee sample. This is a conditional false-positive measurement, not overall product
accuracy. Preserve that transparency; expand independent regression evidence before
claiming reliable automated certification. No such certification claim is warranted.

## What has changed around it

Shopify now distributes eligible catalog information to AI channels and offers catalog
mapping and store knowledge features. Its developer interfaces include Global and
Storefront Catalogs using UCP. This makes basic AI availability or generic catalog cleanup
a weak standalone proposition. It also creates more surfaces that can disagree.

Google already supports product structured data and Merchant Center feeds. Basic schema
validation is therefore not enough differentiation either.

These are platform capabilities, not evidence that every store's buyer requirements are
correct, complete, current or mutually consistent. The opportunity is an inference:
independent tests that find a concrete inconsistency, identify its source and verify a fix.

## A narrower offer to test

**AisleLens helps ecommerce agencies catch product-information failures before their
clients' shoppers or shopping agents encounter them.**

Start with one type of agency and a small set of buyer requirements. Compare storefront
copy, variant/catalog data, structured data and shipping/return policies. Add UCP/MCP
surfaces only where accessible and useful; do not treat lack of access as product failure.
Report the exact disagreement or unsupported answer, where it came from, what can be
changed, and the result of the same test after the agency fixes it.

Existing strengths worth keeping: fixed criteria, traceable evidence, honest unknown
states, repeatable reruns and approval before merchant edits. Generic conversational
models can produce plausible audits cheaply; the value must be reliable evidence and
integration into the agency's release workflow, supported by outcomes customers value.

## Proposed four-week validation, not work already performed

1. Interview five agencies that regularly change catalogs or storefronts. Ask for recent
   defects, time spent, existing tools and who owns the budget. Avoid leading with AI.
2. Run three tightly scoped, permissioned concierge pilots on actual client stores. Keep
   human review; do not silently modify stores or place purchases.
3. Measure actionable verified defects, false alarms, agency review/fix time and demand
   for a repeat audit after a release. Track service costs as well as model costs.
4. Continue only if at least two agencies pay and at least one requests repeat use. These
   are proposed decision thresholds, not market facts. If demand is one-off, consider an
   audit service; if platforms already solve the pain or no one pays, archive it in Labs.

Before outreach: reconcile landing/pricing/privacy/index messaging, verify actual billing
mode and onboarding, and make the buyer task and deliverable understandable in one minute.
No outreach, merchant scans, charges or product-engine changes were made in this review.
